The Spiritual Madness of Mast Qalandar

15 June 2026 · 4 min read · Music Culture

In November 2025, the Etihad Arena hosted a rare collaboration between two musical legends — Sami Yusuf, a British composer known for his work in world music, and Atif Aslam, a Pakistani singer with a magical voice. Two artists from different musical traditions came together for a once-in-a-lifetime spiritual night that takes the listener back to the mystical, almost intoxicated rapture of Sufi devotion.

Older than the arena that held it, older even than the countries that sing it today, Mast Qalandar has carried listeners to that edge for centuries. And only Mast Qalandar could bring that spiritual madness.

A qawwali from the Sufi tradition of the subcontinent, it praises the wandering saint Lal Shahbaz Qalandar of Sindh and voices devotion for Ali ibn Abi Talib. What sounds on the surface like one ecstatic, repeated refrain holds a whole world beneath it: of saints and seekers, of love and longing, of devotion passed from voice to voice.

Dam mast Qalandar mast mast
Dam mast Qalandar mast mast “With every breath — intoxicated with the ecstatic love of the Qalandar.”

To follow its words closely is to step into that world. To sing Mast Qalandar is to chant a holy name until the self loosens, to make every breath an act of devotion, to lose oneself and call that loss a kind of freedom. When Sami Yusuf and Atif Aslam sang it that night in Abu Dhabi, before a crowd separated by language, country, and centuries from the Sindhi shrine the song belongs to, they were not performing a relic — they were surrendering, breath by breath, to an ecstasy that belongs to everyone and to no one.

It does not ask to be understood.
It asks to be felt.
